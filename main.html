Does cost of capital actually constrain renewable deployment?

A transaction-level empirical test of the Calcaterra et al. (2024) cost-of-capital convergence hypothesis, using World Bank PPI, CPI Global Landscape, and IRENA project finance data.

Arun  ·  building on Calcaterra, Aleluia Reis et al., Nature Energy 2024

The core question

What Calcaterra et al. showed

CoC convergence fills ~30% of the NDC→1.5°C renewable electricity gap. IAM ensemble result across 5 models, modeled outputs only.

What this project tests

Does that relationship hold in actual project-level data? Which countries and technologies show CoC as the binding constraint vs. other factors?

Methodology

01

Construct country-technology WACC series from World Bank PPI transaction data (6,000+ deals, 1990–2023), replicating Calcaterra's WACC decomposition at deal level rather than modeled output.

02

Measure deployment gap using IRENA capacity addition data vs. resource-potential benchmarks — controlling for wind/solar irradiance so the CoC signal isn't confounded by geography.

03

Panel regression across country-technology-year observations. Key test: is the CoC → deployment relationship robust after controlling for carbon pricing, political risk, and grid access?

04

Identify binding-constraint countries — where CoC elasticity of deployment exceeds a threshold — to produce a ranked map of where de-risking instruments have the highest marginal impact.
