CoC × Renewables / Arun, STS 2026
Extending Calcaterra et al., Nature Energy 2024

Finance, not physics,
is why renewables stall.

Calcaterra et al. (2024) showed that converging the cost of capital from high-CoC countries (Africa, LatAm, MENA) to OECD levels fills ~30% of the NDC→1.5°C renewable electricity gap — using an ensemble of 5 integrated assessment models.

Their result is modeled. We want to test it empirically — using 6,200+ real project finance transactions from the World Bank PPI database to reconstruct WACC series at the deal level and run the same regression in actual data.

What we're asking Does the CoC → renewable deployment relationship hold in transaction-level data? Which countries have CoC as the binding constraint vs. grid, policy, or resource limits? And can we build a ranked map of where de-risking instruments have the highest marginal impact?

Research methodology

How we extend Calcaterra et al. from IAM outputs to transaction-level empirics. Four steps, all public data.

Step 01
Reconstruct deal-level WACC
Apply Calcaterra et al.'s WACC decomposition (equations 3–4) to individual transactions in the World Bank PPI database. Each deal gets a country risk component (Damodaran default spread), technology risk component (maturity threshold approach), and infrastructure premium — yielding a deal-level WACC estimate we can validate against reported financing terms.
WB PPI database Damodaran risk premia 6,200+ transactions
Step 02
Measure deployment gap
Construct a country-technology-year deployment gap measure: actual capacity additions (IRENA) vs. resource-potential benchmark, controlling for wind speed / solar irradiance (Global Solar Atlas, IRENA) so CoC signal isn't confounded by geography. This gives us a dependent variable that isolates financing constraint from physical constraint.
IRENA capacity stats Global Solar Atlas GWA wind data
Step 03
Panel regression
Country-technology-year panel regression: deployment gap ~ WACC spread + grid quality index (World Bank) + carbon price + political risk (ICRG) + technology maturity FE. Key test: is the CoC coefficient robust and economically meaningful after controls? Does it match the ~30% gap-fill magnitude implied by the IAM ensemble?
Panel OLS / 2SLS ICRG political risk WB governance
Step 04
Binding constraint map
Use regression coefficients to decompose each country's deployment gap into CoC vs. other barriers. Produce a ranked map: which country-technology pairs have CoC elasticity above threshold (i.e., where de-risking instruments have highest marginal impact)? This is the policy-actionable output — a prioritization tool for MDBs and DFIs.
